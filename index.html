Find the best trade-offs, then pick one

Developed by Sheharyaar Hassan, doctoral researcher, FrameBio DC13 · TFTAK / TalTech

Put in a table of options and criteria. The tool removes options that are worse on every criterion (MOO, Multi-Objective Optimisation) and ranks the rest with your priorities (MCDM, Multi-Criteria Decision Making, TOPSIS method). Everything runs in your browser: your data is not sent anywhere.

How it works, and how we know it is correct

Step 1, MOO (Pareto filter). Option B beats option A when B is at least as good on every criterion and strictly better on at least one. Every beaten option is removed. What is left are the best trade-offs: for each of them, nothing better exists. Identical options never beat each other, so copies are all kept.

Step 2, MCDM (TOPSIS). All criteria are put on one scale, multiplied by their weights, and each option is scored by how close it is to the best possible option and how far from the worst.

r = x / √(Σ x²) → v = weight × r → score = D⁻ / (D⁺ + D⁻)

D⁺ is the distance to the best value on every criterion, D⁻ the distance to the worst. A score of 1 is perfect. Scores are relative to the options being ranked: if you add or remove options, the scores change.

Step 3, stability check. The ranking is repeated with thousands of random weightings (an SMAA-type check). If the same option wins most of the time, the choice does not depend much on the exact weights.

Tested. This is the same calculation as our tested Python version (decision_core.py, 30 automatic tests). TOPSIS reproduces all published scores of Bērziņa et al. (2024), Agronomy Research 22(S1), doi:10.15159/AR.24.039, and the Pareto filter finds exactly the 101 true front points of the ZDT1 benchmark (Zitzler, Deb & Thiele 2000). The check at the top of this page re-runs the published example every time the page opens.

Limits. The result is only as good as the numbers you put in. The tool chooses only among the options in your table; it does not invent new ones.
